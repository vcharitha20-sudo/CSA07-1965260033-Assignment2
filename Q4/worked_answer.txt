Q4 – Distance-vector convergence and loops

Converged shortest distances
P: 0 3 1 4 9; Q: 3 0 2 5 10; R: 1 2 0 3 8; S: 4 5 3 0 5; T: 9 10 8 5 0. P reaches Q via R for cost 1+2=3, less than direct cost 4.

Count-to-infinity
When M–N fails in L–M–N (link cost 2), stale advertisements may cause M to believe L still reaches N (cost 4), then L raises its route to 6, and so on until RIP metric 16. Split horizon with poison reverse advertises unreachable metric 16 back to the next hop, preventing this two-router loop; three-router loops can still occur.

Alternative
RIP may rely on its 180-second invalid timer when triggered updates do not resolve the failure; EIGRP may use a feasible successor immediately, otherwise DUAL queries must converge. A feasible successor cannot be asserted without checking each router’s reported distances against its feasible distance.
